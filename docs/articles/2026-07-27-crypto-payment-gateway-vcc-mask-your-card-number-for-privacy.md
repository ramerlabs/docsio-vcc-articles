## How a virtual card masks your real card number

A virtual card is a digital representation of a payment card that has its own 16-digit number, expiration date, and CVV. When you use it at checkout, the merchant sees only that temporary number, not your actual bank or credit card details. The virtual number is linked to your funding source but acts as a shield.

Because the virtual card number is not your primary account number, even if a merchant suffers a data breach, the stolen virtual number cannot be used to access your real account. You can simply close that virtual card and issue a new one. This separation is the core privacy benefit.

## Common mistakes when masking your card with VCCs

- Using the same virtual card for multiple merchants, which defeats the purpose of masking your primary number.
- Forgetting to set a spending limit and later discovering an unexpected charge that exceeds your budget.
- Not checking the merchant's refund policy. If you delete a virtual card before a refund is processed, the refund may fail.
- Assuming all virtual cards are anonymous. Some providers still require KYC verification. Understand your provider's privacy policy.
- Storing virtual card details in plain text in a notes app or email. Always use a password manager or the provider's dashboard.
- Ignoring the virtual card's expiration date. An expired card will cause recurring payments to fail.

## FAQ: Privacy and virtual credit cards

**1. Can merchants see my real name when I use a virtual card?**
No, typically the merchant only sees the virtual card number and the billing name you provide. Your real bank account number is never shared. Some providers allow you to use a pseudonym, but check their terms.

**2. Are virtual cards compatible with all online merchants?**
Most online merchants accept virtual Visa and Mastercard numbers just like physical cards. Some merchants may block prepaid or virtual cards, but major ad platforms like Google Ads and Facebook Ads generally accept them.

**3. Do virtual cards work for international payments?**
Yes, virtual cards work across borders because they use standard card networks. You can pay for services in different currencies without exposing your domestic bank account.

**4. What happens if a merchant tries to charge a virtual card after I delete it?**
The charge will be declined because the virtual card number no longer exists. This is an effective way to stop unwanted recurring payments without contacting the merchant.

**5. Is it legal to use a virtual card to mask my primary card number?**
Yes, using a virtual card is a legitimate payment method offered by regulated financial institutions. It is not illegal to protect your privacy. However, you must still comply with the merchant's terms of service.
